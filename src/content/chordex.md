Traditional harmony imposes absolute labels on fundamentally relative harmonic ideas, making it awkward to work outside the Common Practice Period. Layers of key signatures, Roman numerals, and functional analysis sit between musical intent and emotional result.

Perception is relational. We hear motion—interval, direction, quality—not labels. Those relationships are what give a progression its emotional fingerprint. Chordex models harmony entirely in relative terms, mapping the tertian space as 192 distinct chord-to-chord movements, each with a characteristic affect, from the spicy tension of M ♭II M to the melancholic yearn of m VII M.

These Chord Relationships are indexed with affect tags, scale associations, and audio playback. They can be be searched, filtered, annotated, or stringed together, as a growing personal vocabulary of harmonic gestures. For composers and improvisers, Chordex aims to remove an abstraction layer between intent and sound.
